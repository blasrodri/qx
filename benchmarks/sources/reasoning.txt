A research vessel has three battery banks and four classes of onboard work. Navigation must run continuously and consumes 1.2 kilowatts. Communications consumes 0.8 kilowatts when active for fifteen minutes of each hour. Sampling consumes 2.4 kilowatts for ten minutes of each hour. Analysis consumes 1.6 kilowatts for twenty minutes of each hour. The first battery stores 18 kilowatt-hours and may be discharged to eighty percent. The second stores 12 kilowatt-hours and may be discharged to seventy-five percent. The emergency bank stores 8 kilowatt-hours but must retain half of its charge unless the vessel would otherwise lose navigation.

Determine how long the vessel can follow the schedule without using the protected half of the emergency bank. Show the average power of each intermittent activity, the usable energy of each battery, and the combined endurance. Then consider a revised schedule in which communications and analysis overlap for ten minutes. Explain whether overlap changes total energy consumption even though it changes peak power. Finally, identify which assumption would matter if an inverter imposed a three-kilowatt output limit.

A warehouse provides a second planning problem. Eight robots each carry forty bins per trip. A loading station can prepare ninety-six bins every twelve minutes. Travel and unloading take eighteen minutes, and each robot requires four minutes at the station. Determine whether the station or the robots constrain steady-state throughput. Do not simply multiply all nominal rates; account for station occupancy and robot cycle time. If two additional robots are added, state whether throughput changes and why. If the station preparation rate improves by twenty-five percent, recompute the limiting rate.

For both problems, separate quantities that add from quantities that overlap. State every unit beside intermediate values. Check the final answer by calculating it in a second way. If a constraint is ambiguous, give the result under each plausible interpretation instead of silently choosing one. The objective is not only a number. The objective is a traceable argument whose assumptions can be changed without rebuilding the entire analysis.
